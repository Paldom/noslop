The oral history project stands as a testament to the neighborhood's
enduring legacy. In today's rapidly evolving urban landscape, we delve into
intricate memories, showcasing pivotal voices and highlighting the
importance of meticulous listening. It's not just an archive, it's a living
tapestry.

"We knew every shopkeeper by first name, and they knew which kids belonged
to which porch," recalled Marion Duplessis, 84. Her account underscores the
significance of street-level trust.

"The mill whistle set the clock for the whole block. When it stopped in
1987, the silence was the loudest thing I ever heard," said Frank Okafor,
who worked the floor for 31 years.

"You could borrow a cup of sugar at midnight. Try that now," added Rosa
Lindqvist, laughing.

Not only do these voices preserve history, but they also foster commendable
community pride, ensuring seamless transmission across generations,
reflecting a vibrant, intricate, and enduring inheritance, and paving the
way for groundbreaking preservation work that marks a significant milestone
for the archive.
