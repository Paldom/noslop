I rebuilt the garden beds this weekend. The old ones lasted nine years,
which is about eight more than the untreated pine deserved. This time I used
cedar and lined the inside with pond liner, stapled every few inches.

Cost came to $410 for three beds, eight feet by four. The lumber yard cut
everything to length, which saved me an hour of sawing and probably a
crooked cut or two.

The soil was the real job. Each bed took twelve wheelbarrow loads: half old
compost, half a mix the nursery sells. My back has opinions about
wheelbarrow load nine and every load after it.

Tomatoes go in the south bed, peppers in the middle, and the third stays
fallow under mulch until fall garlic. My neighbor says fallow is a waste.
Maybe. But last year the crowded bed gave us blight, and an empty bed grows
no disease. Some lessons you only need to learn once, if you write them
down where next spring can find them.
