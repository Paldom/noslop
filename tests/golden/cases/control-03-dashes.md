My grandmother kept her recipes on index cards — grease-spotted, corner-torn
cards in a green tin box — and the box outlived her by twenty years before
anyone opened it. When we finally did, the cards were useless in the best
way. "Bake until done." "Enough flour." "The usual oven."

She wasn't hiding anything — she simply never imagined cooking as
instructions. It was a conversation with people who already knew. The cards
were reminders, not recipes — cues for a performance she assumed you'd seen
a hundred times, because everyone she loved had.

I've been trying to cook from them anyway. The bread came out dense the
first time, then denser — my punishment for measuring. The third loaf I
made angry and tired, ignoring the card entirely, and it was hers. Maybe
that's the inheritance — not the instructions, but the permission to stop
following them. The tin sits on my counter now. I add a card sometimes —
"enough garlic" — and let my daughter wonder later.
